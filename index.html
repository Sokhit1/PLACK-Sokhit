Welcome to Air Transport Planning & Management

Welcome ITC engineering students! Aviation is the world's most technologically advanced and globally interconnected transportation mode. In CL01, you will explore how aircraft, aerodromes, navigation corridors, and regulatory frameworks operate cohesively to drive global trade, passenger mobility, and economic modernization.

Course Welcome Message & Academic Context

Instructor: Department of Transportation Engineering, ITC · Academic Year 2025–2026

Why Air Transportation Matters: Aviation accounts for roughly 3.5% of global GDP ($3.5 trillion) and supports over 87 million jobs worldwide. While carrying under 1% of world cargo tonnage, air freight transports over 35% of world trade by economic value ($6.8 trillion annually). Understanding air transport planning is crucial for civil and transportation engineers designing 21st-century multimodal logistics systems.
Relevance for Cambodia: Cambodia is undergoing a historic aviation transformation. With the construction of Techo International Airport (KTI - Code 4F) near Phnom Penh and the opening of Siem Reap-Angkor International Airport (SAI - Code 4E), the nation is positioning itself as a strategic aviation crossroads in ASEAN. Engineers trained at ITC will lead the planning, maintenance, and operation of these mega-facilities.

1.1 Fundamentals of Air Transportation

Economic principles, operational characteristics, speed-distance multipliers, and elasticity

Air transportation is defined as the commercial and operational movement of passengers, mail, and cargo by motorized aircraft through three-dimensional sovereign airspace. Unlike terrestrial modes (highway and rail) which require continuous physical right-of-way infrastructure, air transport operates on a point-to-point network architecture requiring heavy infrastructure capital expenditure only at origin and destination nodes (aerodromes), while relying on invisible electronic navigation corridors in the sky.

Key Definitions:
  • Available Seat Kilometers (ASK): Measure of total airline passenger-carrying capacity calculated by multiplying available aircraft seats by the stage flight distance flown. (Formula: ASK = Seats × Stage Distance).
  • Revenue Passenger Kilometers (RPK): Measure of actual airline traffic volume generated by multiplying fare-paying revenue passengers by distance flown. (Formula: RPK = Paying Passengers × Distance).
  • Passenger Load Factor (PLF): Percentage of available seats filled with revenue passengers. (Formula: PLF = RPK / ASK × 100%). Modern global average PLF sits between 80% and 84%.
  • Value-to-Weight Ratio: The economic criterion governing air cargo adoption. Commodities exceeding $10–$16 per kilogram (such as pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, high-fashion garments) shift to air freight to minimize inventory-holding costs.

Engineering Case Example: The 800-km High-Speed Rail Substitution Threshold

In transportation economics, when high-speed rail (HSR) connects city pairs with door-to-door transit times under 3.5 hours (typically distances under 800 km, such as Beijing–Shanghai or Paris–Lyon), airlines lose between 60% and 80% of passenger market share because rail eliminates airport check-in, TSA/security queues, and suburban transit delays. However, for distances above 1,000 km, or across bodies of water (such as Phnom Penh to Singapore or Bangkok to Manila), jet aviation remains completely dominant.

Why do Cambodian garment manufacturers and electronics assembly plants in special economic zones (SEZs) choose to export finished garments via sea freight from Sihanoukville Autonomous Port, while importing microchips and high-value fabric components via air freight at Phnom Penh / Techo International Airport?

1.2 History and Evolution of Aviation

From the Wright Brothers and DC-3 propliners to widebody turbofans and deregulation

Aviation transitioned from an experimental novelty into the backbone of global commerce through five technological and economic epochs. Each stage was sparked by engineering breakthroughs in aerodynamics, propulsion thermodynamics, materials science, and government regulatory reform.

1903–1935 Pioneering & Early Airmail

Wright Flyer (1903, Kitty Hawk). Governments subsidized early aviation through lucrative airmail postal contracts (Kelly Act of 1925), financing the creation of early navigation beacons and scheduled carriers.

1936–1955 The DC-3 & Post-WWII Piston Era

The Douglas DC-3 (1936) became the first airliner capable of operating profitably on passenger fares alone without postal subsidies. Post-war surplus aircraft (C-47, Lockheed Constellation) seeded global international airlines.

1958–1977 The Commercial Jet Age

Boeing 707 (1958) and DC-8 introduced gas-turbine jet engines, slashing transatlantic transit from 14 hours to 7 hours. In 1970, the Boeing 747 'Jumbo Jet' democratization mass travel with twin-aisle widebody economics.

1978–2000 Deregulation & Low-Cost Carriers

US Airline Deregulation Act of 1978 eliminated government price-fixing and route protection. Unleashed free-market competition, Hub-and-Spoke networks, and pioneered the Southwest/Ryanair Low-Cost Carrier (LCC) business model.

2000–Present Ultra-Efficient Twinjets & Carbon Transition

Carbon-composite airframes (Boeing 787 Dreamliner, Airbus A350) and ultra-high-bypass turbofans (LEAP, Trent XWB) enabling point-to-point long-haul flying while targeting net-zero 2050 via Sustainable Aviation Fuels (SAF).

The 1944 Chicago Convention: Birth of International Civil Aviation

In November 1944, delegates from 54 nations gathered in Chicago to draft the Convention on International Civil Aviation. This landmark treaty established: (1) Sovereign state jurisdiction over national airspace, (2) The creation of ICAO (International Civil Aviation Organization), and (3) The standard framework of the Freedoms of the Air, regulating commercial overflight and landing rights.

Discussion Question:

Why did quad-engine airliners like the Boeing 747 and Airbus A380 get phased out in favor of twin-engine widebodies like the Boeing 787 and Airbus A350, despite having higher passenger capacity?

1.3 Components of Air Transportation Systems

Aircraft, Aerodromes (Airside vs Landside), Air Navigation Services (ANSPs), and Operations

An air transportation system is a tightly coupled sociotechnical ecosystem comprised of four macro-components:

1. Aircraft (Vehicles)

Commercial narrowbody (A320/B737) and widebody (B777/A350) aircraft certified under airworthiness regulations (FAR Part 25 / CS-25). Governed by aerodynamics (Lift, Weight, Thrust, Drag), payload-range curves, and maximum takeoff weight (MTOW).

2. Aerodromes (Nodes)

Fixed ground infrastructure divided strictly into Airside (runways, taxiways, holding bays, aprons, control tower) and Landside (passenger terminals, curbside pickup, baggage claim, road/rail transit links).

3. Air Navigation (ANSP)

Air Traffic Management (ATM), Communication, Navigation, and Surveillance (CNS) systems. Includes Area Control Centers (ACC), terminal approach radar, Instrument Landing Systems (ILS CAT I/II/III), and ADS-B satellite tracking.

4. Operations & Enterprises

Scheduled airlines, maintenance repair and overhaul (MRO) facilities, ground handling agents (baggage, catering, ramp marshalling), customs & immigration (CIQ), and fuel suppliers.

Airside vs. Landside Boundary Separation

Airside (Restricted Security Zone)
  • Runways & Blast Pads (Takeoff and landing maneuvers)
  • Taxiway Systems (Parallel taxiways, rapid-exit taxiways)
  • Aprons / Ramps (Aircraft parking stands, jet bridges)
  • Control Tower (Tower Cab & visual ground controllers)
  • Aircraft Rescue and Fire Fighting (ARFF Station)
Landside (Public Commercial Area)
  • Passenger Departure & Check-In Concourse
  • Security Screening Checkpoints & Customs/CIQ
  • Retail Concessions, Duty-Free & Food Beverage
  • Baggage Claim Carousels & Ground Arrivals
  • Multimodal Curbside, Parking Garages & Rail Terminals

1.4 Classification of Air Transportation

Commercial Aviation (FSC vs LCC), Air Cargo Operations, and General Aviation

Civil aviation is categorized by ICAO and regulatory authorities into three primary operational branches:

Commercial Air Transport (CAT) Scheduled Passenger Carriers

Scheduled airlines offering passenger transportation for compensation or hire. Divided into two predominant business models:

  • Full-Service Carriers (FSCs): Singapore Airlines, Qatar Airways, Vietnam Airlines. Complex multi-class cabins, airline alliances (Star Alliance, Oneworld), hub-and-spoke feed, and cargo belly-hold monetization.
  • Low-Cost Carriers (LCCs): AirAsia Cambodia, Ryanair, Cebu Pacific. Single-fleet type (A320 or B737), high aircraft daily utilization (>12 hrs/day), point-to-point secondary airports, unbundled ancillary fees.
Logistics & Trade Air Cargo & Express Freight

Carrying freight, courier express, and mail. Operates through two delivery models:

  • Belly-Hold Cargo: Utilizing the unutilized underfloor volume of passenger aircraft (carries >50% of global air tonnage). Highly cost-effective marginal revenue for passenger airlines.
  • Main-Deck Freighters & Integrators: FedEx, UPS, DHL, Cargolux. Operating dedicated cargo aircraft (Boeing 777F, 747-8F) with standardized Unit Load Devices (ULDs) on nighttime hub-and-spoke express schedules.
Non-Scheduled / Private General Aviation (GA) & Aerial Work

All civil aviation operations other than scheduled commercial transport and military defense:

  • Business & Corporate Aviation: Private jets (Gulfstream, Bombardier, Dassault) providing point-to-point on-demand executive transport.
  • Instructional Flying: Flight training academies and universities training licensed pilots.
  • Aerial Work: Emergency medical evacuation (Medevac), aerial crop dusting, firefighting, geographical surveying, and humanitarian relief.

1.5 Aviation Organizations and Regulations

ICAO, IATA, Civil Aviation Authorities (CAAs), and Cambodia SSCA

Global aviation functions seamlessly across international borders because of a three-tier regulatory and institutional hierarchy:

ICAO
International Civil Aviation Organization United Nations Specialized Agency · Montreal, Canada

Intergovernmental body of 193 member states. Formulates the 19 Annexes to the Chicago Convention containing SARPs (Standards and Recommended Practices) for aerodrome design (Annex 14), air traffic services (Annex 11), environmental protection (Annex 16), and aviation security (Annex 17).

Legal Mandate: Global International Standards
IATA
International Air Transport Association Airline Trade Association · Montreal / Geneva

Non-governmental trade organization representing ~320 commercial airlines accounting for 83% of total world air traffic. Manages international ticket clearinghouses (BSP), three-letter airport codes (PNH, SAI, SIN), Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR), and IOSA safety audits.

Legal Mandate: Commercial Trade body (No Sovereign Police Power)
SSCA
State Secretariat of Civil Aviation Cambodia Sovereign Aviation Regulator · Phnom Penh

The sovereign regulatory authority of the Royal Government of Cambodia. Enforces civil aviation law, issues Air Operator Certificates (AOC), certifies aerodromes (KTI, PNH, SAI, KOS), licenses flight crew and engineers, and conducts safety oversight over Cambodian airspace.

Legal Mandate: Sovereign National Airspace Law Enforcement

1.6 Airport Networks and Aviation Development in Cambodia

Techo (KTI), Siem Reap-Angkor (SAI), Sihanoukville (KOS), and National Air Connectivity

Cambodia's civil aviation sector is experiencing unprecedented growth driven by the national economic integration strategy within ASEAN. The kingdom's aviation infrastructure is anchored by three primary international commercial gateways and domestic airfields:

Techo International Airport (KTI) ICAO Code 4F
Location: Kandal / Takeo Provinces (Phnom Penh South) · Greenfield Hub
  • Runway: 4,000 m × 60 m · CAT III-B instrument capability
  • Aircraft Capability: Full capability for Airbus A380-800, Boeing 777-9, and 747-8F
  • Capacity: Phase 1: 13 million passengers/year → Phase 3: 50 million passengers/year
  • Master Plan: Greenfield aerodrome designed by Foster + Partners with intermodal express transit connection to Phnom Penh center
Siem Reap-Angkor Int'l Airport (SAI) ICAO Code 4E
Location: Soutr Nikom District, Siem Reap · Opened late 2023
  • Runway: 3,600 m × 45 m · CAT II instrument approach
  • Aircraft Capability: Accommodates long-haul widebodies (Airbus A350, Boeing 787)
  • Capacity: Phase 1: 7 million passengers/year → Phase 2: 12 million passengers/year
  • Heritage Protection: Located 40 km east of Angkor Wat to prevent structural sonic and vibration damage to world heritage temples
Sihanoukville Int'l Airport (KOS) ICAO Code 4E Extended
Location: Preah Sihanouk Province · Coastal Trade Gateway
  • Runway: Extended to 3,300 m to accommodate widebody tourist and cargo traffic
  • Economic Role: Serves the coastal tourism corridor and Preah Sihanouk Multi-Purpose Special Economic Zone (SEZ)
  • Logistics Integration: Seamless intermodal linkage with Sihanoukville Deep Sea Autonomous Port and Phnom Penh–Sihanoukville Expressway

Core Engineering Takeaways from CL01

1. Point-to-Point Node Advantage

Air transportation avoids the immense capital costs of continuous physical ground corridors, focusing engineering investment solely on origin and destination aerodromes and high-tech airspace management.

2. Air Cargo Economic Asymmetry

While accounting for under 1% of global freight tonnage, air cargo transports over 35% of world trade by economic value ($6.8 trillion), driven by high value-to-weight and time-sensitive commodities.

3. Strict Airside vs Landside Zoning

Aerodrome planning strictly separates restricted security aeronautical movement areas (runways, taxiways, aprons) from public passenger processing concourses and curbside multimodal hubs.

4. Three-Tier Governance Synergy

Global civil aviation relies on ICAO (UN standards/SARPs), IATA (commercial airline clearinghouse and trade standards), and sovereign state regulators like Cambodia SSCA enforcing national safety law.

5. Cambodia's Strategic Infrastructure

Techo International Airport (KTI - Code 4F) and Siem Reap-Angkor (SAI - Code 4E) provide modern, widebody-capable capacity that establishes Cambodia as an emerging intermodal hub within ASEAN.

6. Quantitative Operational KPIs

Key metrics mastered: ASK (Capacity = Seats × Distance), RPK (Traffic = Revenue Pax × Distance), and PLF (Load Factor = RPK / ASK × 100%).

Essential Aviation Vocabulary Reference

ASK (Available Seat Km):
The total passenger carrying capacity of an airline stage (Seats × km).
RPK (Revenue Passenger Km):
The total volume of fare-paying passenger traffic (Paying Pax × km).
SARPs:
Standards and Recommended Practices adopted by ICAO across its 19 Annexes.
Code 4F Aerodrome:
ICAO aerodrome classification accommodating aircraft with reference field length ≥ 1,800m and wingspan 65m–80m (A380, B777-9).
CAT III-B ILS:
Precision Instrument Landing System allowing approaches with decision height < 50 ft and runway visual range down to 75m.
SSCA:
State Secretariat of Civil Aviation: Cambodia's sovereign civil aviation regulatory authority.

CL02 / Overview

Air Transport Demand and Forecasting

Passenger demand · influencing factors · growth rate modeling · regression & econometric forecasting

Curriculum & Core Competencies

  • Explain macroeconomic and demographic drivers of air passenger growth (GDP elasticity).
  • Compute Compound Annual Growth Rates (CAGR) for airport passenger terminals.
  • Apply multi-variable linear regression models to predict origin-destination traffic.
  • Construct Baseline, Optimistic, and Conservative traffic scenario forecasts.

Resources / Acronyms

Aviation Acronym Directory

Standard international abbreviations across air transport planning, operations, and policy
Acronym Full Term Functional Domain & Context
ICAO International Civil Aviation Organization UN specialized agency establishing global aviation SARPs (Chicago Convention).
IATA International Air Transport Association Trade association representing ~300 airlines; sets ticketing, baggage, and operational standards.
SSCA State Secretariat of Civil Aviation Cambodia's civil aviation regulatory authority overseeing safety, security, and air transport.
ASK Available Seat Kilometers Measure of airline passenger carrying capacity (Seats × Distance flown).
RPK Revenue Passenger Kilometers Measure of passenger traffic volume (Paying passengers × Distance flown).
PLF Passenger Load Factor Ratio of RPK to ASK expressing percentage of passenger seating capacity filled.
CASK Cost per Available Seat Kilometer Unit operating cost incurred per seat flown one kilometer.
RASK Revenue per Available Seat Kilometer Unit operating revenue generated per seat flown one kilometer.
OTP On-Time Performance Punctuality metric measuring flights arriving or departing within 15 minutes of schedule.
MTOW Maximum Takeoff Weight Maximum certified gross weight at which aircraft is cleared to take off.
MLW Maximum Landing Weight Maximum structural limit for landing without structural damage or tire failure.
OEW Operating Empty Weight Basic weight of aircraft including crew, fluids, and equipment, excluding payload and fuel.
ATC Air Traffic Control Service managing aircraft ground and airspace movements to maintain separation.
ATM Air Traffic Management Integrated system of air traffic services, airspace management, and air traffic flow management.
FIR Flight Information Region Designated airspace within which flight information and alerting services are provided.
AODB Airport Operational Database Central repository of operational flight schedules, gate assignments, and resource usage.
BHS Baggage Handling System Mechanical and sorting infrastructure transporting checked luggage from check-in to aircraft.
FIDS Flight Information Display System Passenger-facing digital monitors displaying arrival, departure, and gate statuses.
LOS Level of Service IATA quality metric rating passenger terminal comfort and spatial density (A to F).
SAF Sustainable Aviation Fuel Bio-based or synthetic drop-in aviation fuel reducing lifecycle carbon emissions by up to 80%.
CORSIA Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation ICAO market-based measure to stabilize international aviation net CO₂ emissions.
SARPs Standards and Recommended Practices Technical specifications adopted by ICAO Council pursuant to Article 37 of Chicago Convention.
