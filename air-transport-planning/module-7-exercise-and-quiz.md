## The risk matrix

Risk score = **Likelihood × Severity**, each scored 1–5.

| Likelihood ↓ / Severity → | 1 Negligible | 2 Minor | 3 Major | 4 Hazardous | 5 Catastrophic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 Frequent | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
| 4 Occasional | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
| 3 Remote | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 15 |
| 2 Improbable | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
| 1 Extremely improbable | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |

- **1–4 Low:** acceptable, monitor
- **5–9 Medium:** tolerable with mitigation
- **10–25 High:** intolerable, act before operations continue

*Simplified teaching version of the ICAO Safety Management Manual matrix; score bands are a teaching convention.*

**Hierarchy of controls (most to least effective):** eliminate → engineer → procedures → training → protective equipment.

## Classroom exercise: assess five airside hazards

*Group work, about 30 minutes. Scores are illustrative.*

| Hazard | Likelihood | Severity | Score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A · FOD on a taxiway after construction work | 4 | 3 | ? |
| B · Bird activity near the runway in the rainy season | 3 | 4 | ? |
| C · Unescorted contractor found on the apron | 3 | 3 | ? |
| D · Fuel spill during refuelling | 2 | 4 | ? |
| E · Baggage cart left unsecured on a stand | 4 | 2 | ? |

### Tasks

1. Score and classify each hazard.
2. Rank them by priority.
3. Decide whether each is a safety or a security issue.
4. Propose controls using the hierarchy of controls.

---

## Solution

| Hazard | Score | Zone | Type | Strongest controls |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| A · FOD after works | 4 × 3 = **12** | **High** | Safety | Inspect before reopening; contractor housekeeping rules |
| B · Birds near runway | 3 × 4 = **12** | **High** | Safety | Drain standing water; patrols and dispersal |
| C · Unescorted contractor | 3 × 3 = 9 | Medium | Security | Enforce escort rules; check permits at gates |
| D · Fuel spill | 2 × 4 = 8 | Medium | Safety | Procedures, spill kits, fire cover on standby |
| E · Unsecured cart | 4 × 2 = 8 | Medium | Safety | Brakes and chocks; stand checks before arrival |

**Priority:** act on A and B before operations continue; reduce C, D and E as far as reasonably practicable. Good answers use elimination and engineering before procedures and training, and name an owner for each control.

## Review quiz

1. A bird strike during take-off is mainly a…
   A. Safety hazard · B. Security threat · C. Capacity problem
2. Likelihood 4 and severity 4: what is the score and zone?
   A. 8, medium · B. 16, high · C. 4, low
3. Which control is usually the most effective?
   A. Protective equipment · B. Training · C. Eliminating the hazard
4. Which ICAO Annex covers aviation security?
   A. Annex 14 · B. Annex 17 · C. Annex 19
5. Which SMS pillar includes hazard identification?
   A. Safety policy · B. Safety risk management · C. Safety promotion

<details>
<summary><b>Answers</b></summary>

1. **A** — an unintended event, so safety
2. **B** — 4 × 4 = 16, high zone
3. **C** — elimination is at the top of the hierarchy of controls
4. **B** — Annex 17 (Annex 14 aerodromes, Annex 19 safety management)
5. **B** — safety risk management

</details>
