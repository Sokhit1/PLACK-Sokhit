## Worked example: one flight

180 seats · 1,000 km · 153 passengers · average fare $120 · flight cost $19,800

| Metric | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| ASK | 180 × 1,000 | 180,000 |
| RPK | 153 × 1,000 | 153,000 |
| Load factor | 153,000 ÷ 180,000 | 85.0% |
| Revenue | 153 × $120 | $18,360 |
| Yield | 18,360 ÷ 153,000 | $0.120 |
| CASK | 19,800 ÷ 180,000 | $0.110 |
| Break-even load factor | 0.110 ÷ 0.120 | 91.7% |

**Result:** a loss of $1,440 (18,360 − 19,800). The flight needs about 165 passengers to break even.

## Classroom exercise: choose the fleet for Route X

*Group work, about 25 minutes. All numbers are invented for teaching.*

Route X: **300 passengers per day each way**, 800 km, average fare **$110**.

| Option | Aircraft | Flights per day | Trip cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A | 180 seats | 2 | $14,000 |
| B | 70 seats | 4 | $5,600 |

Work per direction, per day. Passengers carried = the smaller of demand and seats offered.

### Tasks

1. Seats offered and passengers carried.
2. Load factor for each option.
3. Revenue, cost and profit.
4. Recommend an option and explain the trade-offs.

---

## Solution

| Per direction, per day | Option A · 2 × 180 seats | Option B · 4 × 70 seats |
|---|---:|---:|
| Seats offered | 360 | 280 |
| Passengers carried | 300 | 280 (20 spilled) |
| Load factor | 83% | 100% |
| Revenue | 300 × $110 = $33,000 | 280 × $110 = $30,800 |
| Cost | 2 × $14,000 = $28,000 | 4 × $5,600 = $22,400 |
| **Profit** | **$5,000** | **$8,400** |

**Recommendation.** Option B earns more and its four daily flights attract time-sensitive business travellers (frequency wins market share). But it already runs 100% full: 20 passengers a day are lost to competitors and there is no room for growth. Recommend B now, monitor spill, and add a fifth frequency or a larger aircraft as demand grows. Seat cost alone ($77.8 for A vs $80 for B) would have chosen A, which shows why right-sizing matters.

## Review quiz

1. A 180-seat flight of 1,000 km carries 144 passengers. What is its load factor?
   A. 80% · B. 72% · C. 125%
2. Which is a typical low-cost carrier feature?
   A. Several cabins and lounges · B. One aircraft family and paid extras · C. Hub with connecting banks
3. How many routes does a full point-to-point network of 8 cities need?
   A. 7 · B. 28 · C. 56
4. CASK is $0.09 and yield is $0.12. What is the break-even load factor?
   A. 67% · B. 75% · C. 133%
5. Which option adds short-term capacity with crew included?
   A. Purchase · B. Operating lease · C. Wet lease (ACMI)

<details>
<summary><b>Answers</b></summary>

1. **A** — 144 ÷ 180 = 80% (distance cancels out)
2. **B** — one aircraft family and paid extras
3. **B** — 8 × 7 ÷ 2 = 28 routes
4. **B** — 0.09 ÷ 0.12 = 75%
5. **C** — a wet lease (ACMI) includes aircraft, crew, maintenance and insurance

</details>
