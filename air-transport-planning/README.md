## Module 4 · Airport Capacity and Level of Service

**CLO4:** Evaluate airport capacity and passenger Level of Service by analyzing airside and terminal facilities, identifying operational bottlenecks, and proposing appropriate improvement measures.

**Learning objectives.** Students should be able to:

1. Define airport capacity.
2. Explain the relationship between airport demand and capacity.
3. Analyze runway and terminal capacity.
4. Calculate a basic demand-to-capacity ratio.
5. Identify airport bottlenecks.
6. Evaluate passenger Level of Service.
7. Recommend operational and infrastructure improvements.

**Outline (33 slides)**

- Introduction, definition and importance of airport capacity
- Demand versus capacity; types of capacity
- Airside: runway, taxiway, apron and aircraft stands
- Terminal: passenger process chain, check-in, security, immigration, boarding gates, baggage claim
- Landside: ground access
- Peak-hour demand, demand-to-capacity ratio, bottlenecks, congestion and delay
- Level of Service: indicators, waiting time and queues, space and comfort, walking distance and accessibility
- Capacity and operational improvement strategies; smart airport technologies
- Classroom exercise, review quiz, summary

**Key formulas**

- Demand-to-capacity ratio: `D/C = Demand ÷ Capacity`
- Processor capacity: `service points × 3,600 ÷ processing time per passenger (s)`
- Runway capacity: `≈ 3,600 ÷ average time between movements (s)`
- Stands required: `peak-hour arrivals × average stand occupancy time ÷ utilization factor`
- Little's law: `queue length = arrival rate × average waiting time`

> Airport Performance = Capacity + Demand + Delay + Level of Service

## Module 5 · Airline Planning and Management

**CLO5:** Evaluate airline market demand, business models, route structures, and fleet options, and recommend suitable planning and management strategies to improve operational and commercial performance.

**Learning objectives.** Students should be able to:

1. Explain the drivers and segments of air travel demand.
2. Estimate demand with a simple forecasting model.
3. Compare airline business models and cost structures.
4. Calculate ASK, RPK, load factor, yield and CASK.
5. Evaluate point-to-point and hub-and-spoke networks.
6. Select suitable aircraft for a route and fleet.
7. Recommend operational and commercial strategies.

**Outline (31 slides)**

- Airline planning horizons and the planning process
- Market demand: drivers, segments, forecasting, gravity model, frequency and market share
- Business models: full-service, low-cost and others; cost structure
- Performance metrics with a worked one-flight example
- Route structures: point-to-point, hub-and-spoke, route evaluation, freedoms of the air
- Fleet planning: process, selection criteria, aircraft comparison, trip versus seat cost, ownership options
- Strategies: schedule planning, revenue management, operational and commercial performance
- Classroom exercise, review quiz, summary

**Key formulas**

- Load factor: `RPK ÷ ASK`
- Yield: `passenger revenue ÷ RPK`
- CASK: `operating cost ÷ ASK`
- Break-even load factor: `CASK ÷ yield`
- Gravity model: `T(ij) = k × P(i) × P(j) ÷ d(ij)ⁿ`
- Aircraft needed: `daily block hours required ÷ utilization per aircraft + spares`

> Airline Profit = ASK × (Load factor × Yield − CASK)

## Module 6 · Airline Operations and Financial Performance

**CLO6:** Evaluate flight schedules, aircraft utilization, operating costs, revenue performance, and route profitability, and recommend strategies to improve airline operational and financial performance.

**Learning objectives.** Students should be able to:

1. Explain the objectives and constraints of scheduling.
2. Evaluate schedules with block time and punctuality.
3. Calculate aircraft utilization and its cost effect.
4. Classify operating costs and build a trip cost.
5. Measure revenue with yield, RASK and ancillaries.
6. Analyze route profitability and break-even load.
7. Recommend operational and financial strategies.

**Outline (30 slides)**

- From flight schedule to profit; recap of key measures
- Flight schedules: objectives and constraints, schedule patterns, block time and buffers, schedule indicators
- Aircraft utilization: definition and typical ranges, effect on unit cost, the turnaround critical path, limits
- Operating costs: classification by driver, building a trip cost, CASK and stage length, fuel price sensitivity
- Revenue: sources, measures (yield, PRASK, RASK, ancillary), load factor versus revenue per seat
- Route profitability: contribution ladder, break-even analysis, route decision grid
- Performance dashboard; operational and financial improvement strategies
- Classroom exercise, review quiz, summary, thank you

**Key formulas**

- Utilization: `block hours ÷ (aircraft × days)`
- Trip cost: `Σ (cost driver × rate)` per block hour, per flight and per passenger
- PRASK: `load factor × yield`
- Break-even passengers: `trip cost ÷ revenue per passenger`
- Operating margin: `(revenue − cost) ÷ revenue`

> Operating Margin = (RASK − CASK) ÷ RASK
