## Key formulas

- CO₂ from fuel: **CO₂ (kg) = fuel burned (kg) × 3.16**
- CO₂ per passenger-km = CO₂ ÷ passengers ÷ distance (km)
- SAF blend saving = blend share × lifecycle saving (e.g. 10% × 80% = 8%)
- Combined saving = 1 − (1 − s₁) × (1 − s₂) × (1 − s₃) — savings multiply, they do not add
- Intensity indicators: kWh ÷ passengers · litres ÷ passengers · recycled ÷ total waste

**Worked example.** A 900 km flight with 150 passengers burns 2,500 kg of fuel → 2,500 × 3.16 = 7,900 kg CO₂ → 52.7 kg per passenger → about 59 g per passenger-km. *(Illustrative.)*

## Classroom exercise: cut a route's emissions

*Group work, about 30 minutes. Figures are illustrative.*

An airline's domestic route burns **20,000 kg of fuel per day**. Use 3.16 kg CO₂ per kg of fuel.

| Option | Measure | Effect |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Replace aircraft with a new-generation type | −15% fuel |
| B | Blend 10% SAF (80% lifecycle saving) | ? % CO₂ |
| C | Single-engine taxi, continuous descent, less weight | −3% fuel |

### Tasks

1. Calculate daily CO₂ today.
2. Calculate the CO₂ saved by A, B and C alone.
3. Calculate the combined saving of all three.
4. Recommend a strategy and justify it using the criteria: CO₂ saved, cost per tonne, time to deliver, local side effects, who pays and who benefits.

---

## Solution

| Step | Calculation | CO₂ per day |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 · Today | 20,000 × 3.16 | 63,200 kg |
| 2A · New aircraft saves | 63,200 × 15% | 9,480 kg |
| 2B · 10% SAF saves | 63,200 × 8% | 5,056 kg |
| 2C · Operations save | 63,200 × 3% | 1,896 kg |
| 3 · All three combined | 1 − 0.85 × 0.92 × 0.97 = 24.1% | **15,231 kg** |

Remaining ≈ **47,969 kg CO₂ per day**. Simply adding 15 + 8 + 3 = 26% overstates the saving, because each measure acts on what is left after the others.

**A sound recommendation:** start with C (cheap, quick, saves cost too); plan A to coincide with fleet renewal (largest cut); grow B as SAF supply and price allow.

## Review quiz

1. A flight burns 3,000 kg of fuel. How much CO₂ does it emit?
   A. 3,000 kg · B. 6,320 kg · C. 9,480 kg
2. Electricity bought for the terminal falls under which scope?
   A. Scope 1 · B. Scope 2 · C. Scope 3
3. Which lever gives the largest share of the industry's net-zero plan?
   A. Hydrogen aircraft · B. Offsets · C. Sustainable aviation fuel
4. In ICAO's balanced approach to noise, operating restrictions are…
   A. The first step · B. A last resort · C. Not allowed
5. Measures cutting 10% and 20% combine to save…
   A. 28% · B. 30% · C. 2%

<details>
<summary><b>Answers</b></summary>

1. **C** — 3,000 × 3.16 = 9,480 kg
2. **B** — purchased electricity is Scope 2
3. **C** — SAF, about 65% in IATA's net-zero roadmap
4. **B** — restrictions such as curfews come after reduction at source, land-use planning and operating procedures
5. **A** — 1 − 0.9 × 0.8 = 0.28, so 28%

</details>
